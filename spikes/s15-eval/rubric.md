# What makes one launch film better than another

A launch film sells a real product in 15–45 s, cut to music, with the product's own screens as the hero.
Judge the film, not the product: the same recording is behind both versions.

1. **The product is the subject.** The thing that matters on screen is big, sharp, centred and readable. A
   frame that's mostly empty page, or a tiny full-page screenshot, is worse.
2. **It moves like a designed film, not a screen recording.** Camera pushes, lifted and tilted windows,
   pieces of the UI arriving on their own, transitions that carry the eye. Long stretches of a still page
   are worse. Motion that is busy but goes nowhere is also worse.
3. **Type is legible and confident.** Words are big, short, well placed, never cut off or covering what
   they point at. Contrast is high enough to read in a thumbnail.
4. **Rhythm.** Changes land on the music. A move doesn't repeat back to back; nothing holds too long.
5. **Craft.** No glitches: no black frames, broken layouts, stretched or blurry footage, stray cursors,
   pages caught half-loaded, or text running off the frame.
6. **Story.** It opens on the product's idea, shows a few features with proof, and ends on the brand.

When the two are close, say so: "same" is a valid answer.
